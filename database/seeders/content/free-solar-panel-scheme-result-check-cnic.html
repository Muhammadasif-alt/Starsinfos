What Is the CM Punjab Free Solar Panel Scheme?

The CM Punjab Free Solar Panel Scheme is a Punjab government initiative intended to provide free solar systems to selected domestic electricity consumers. The scheme aimed to support households using up to 200 electricity units per month, subject to the programme’s eligibility requirements and verification.

According to the Punjab Energy Department, the programme had a financial outlay of approximately Rs 9.97 billion and a target of up to 100,000 beneficiaries. Applicants were to be selected through transparent computerized balloting, followed by physical verification.

The scheme was designed around the electricity consumption recorded for June 2024. Therefore, a household’s current monthly usage alone does not necessarily establish whether it qualified for the original programme.

Is Registration Open in 2026?

The Punjab Portal lists January 5, 2025, as the last date for registration. The official scheme pages reviewed for this article do not confirm a new registration round. The original registration process should therefore be treated as closed unless the government announces otherwise on an official channel.

This distinction matters because some websites, social media posts, or agents may advertise a “new registration” or ask for payment to add a name to the list. Do not pay anyone or submit sensitive information to an unofficial website based on such claims.

If you already applied during the original registration window, you can use the official portal to look for the ballot result or contact the scheme’s official support channel for guidance. Checking a result is different from submitting a new application.

What Do You Need Before Checking the Result?

Prepare the following information before opening the portal:

  1. Electricity bill reference number: Find it on the electricity bill for the connection used for the original application. Use the number associated with the relevant meter and application.
  2. CNIC: Keep the applicant’s Computerized National Identity Card available in case it is needed for verification or support.
  3. Internet access: Use a reliable connection and open the official portal directly.
  4. A clear copy of the bill: A printed bill or a readable digital copy can help you confirm the reference number.
  5. Application or SMS record: If you registered by SMS or saved a confirmation message, keep it for reference.

Do not assume that entering a CNIC alone will always return the result. The result page may require the electricity bill reference number and a security check such as a CAPTCHA. Follow the fields actually displayed on the official website.

Step-by-Step: Check the Free Solar Panel Scheme Result Online

Step 1: Open the official portal

Open the official website at cmsolarscheme.punjab.gov.pk. Check the domain carefully before entering any information. Avoid sponsored search results or unfamiliar sites that imitate government pages.

Step 2: Find the result-checking option

Look for an option labelled “Application Result,” “Ballot Result,” or similar wording on the portal. The exact layout may change, so use the labels shown on the live website rather than relying on an old screenshot or tutorial.

Step 3: Enter the requested bill details

If the result form asks for an electricity bill reference number, enter it carefully. Use the reference number for the connection associated with your application. Do not substitute a meter number, consumer name, or CNIC unless the form specifically requests that field.

Step 4: Complete the security check

If a CAPTCHA or another verification step appears, complete it exactly as shown. If the characters are unclear, refresh the CAPTCHA if that option is available.

Step 5: Submit the form

Select the button to submit or check the result. Wait for the page to load before submitting again. Repeated attempts with incorrect information may not help and can make the process more confusing.

Step 6: Read the result carefully

Review the result displayed for your electricity connection. Save a screenshot or note the result for your records, but avoid sharing a screenshot publicly if it displays your personal or electricity account information.

If the page does not show a result, do not automatically assume you were rejected. First check whether the reference number is correct, whether the portal is working, and whether the official support channel can clarify the issue.

Can You Check the Result by CNIC?

The phrase “check by CNIC” is widely used in searches, but it can be misleading if it suggests that a CNIC-only search is guaranteed to work. The official Punjab Portal explains that the original registration process used the electricity reference number and CNIC, including an SMS method in which applicants sent the reference number and CNIC to 8800 during the registration period.

For online ballot-result checking, follow the current official portal’s form. If it asks for the electricity bill reference number, use that number. Keep the applicant’s CNIC ready in case the portal or support team requests it for verification.

Important: Do not send your CNIC number to random WhatsApp numbers, social media accounts, or unofficial websites that claim they can reveal the result. Use only the official portal or an officially listed support channel.

Can You Check the Result Through SMS to 8800?

During the original registration period, the Punjab government advertised an SMS registration route using the electricity reference number and CNIC sent to 8800. That was a registration method, not proof that a fresh application can be created today.

Because the original registration deadline has passed and a new SMS result-checking service was not confirmed in the official information reviewed for this guide, do not assume that sending a new SMS to 8800 will register you or provide a current result. For a result check, start with the official web portal and follow its current instructions.

If you still have a previous confirmation or notification message, keep it. If you need help interpreting it, contact the scheme through an official support channel rather than replying to an unverified message.

Who Was Eligible for the Original Scheme?

The official Punjab Portal and Energy Department describe the main eligibility conditions for the original scheme. The criteria included:

  • The applicant was a domestic electricity consumer in Punjab.
  • Electricity consumption was up to 200 units per month, assessed using the June 2024 bill.
  • The sanctioned load was up to 2 kilowatts.
  • The applicant met the scheme’s meter, payment, and other eligibility conditions.

The programme was not an unrestricted offer for every household. Meeting the usage threshold alone did not guarantee selection because beneficiaries were chosen through computerized balloting and then subject to verification.

Who Was Excluded?

The Punjab Portal lists several exclusions for the original programme. These included domestic consumers associated with electricity theft, defective meters, multiple meters at a single residence, and payment defaults involving more than three late payments in a 12-month period.

The precise assessment depended on the official programme rules and verification process. If your household’s circumstances were unusual, do not rely only on a general online summary. Refer to the official scheme information or ask the relevant support team for clarification.

What Solar System Was Offered?

The government’s original scheme announcement described different system sizes based on electricity consumption. The announced allocation included 550-watt systems for eligible households using up to 100 units and 1,100-watt systems for eligible households using 101–200 units, subject to the programme’s terms.

These figures describe the original scheme design; they do not guarantee that every applicant will receive a system. Selection by ballot and successful physical verification were important parts of the process. The official programme information should be used for any final confirmation about an individual household’s allocation.

An error may be caused by a mistyped reference number, a temporary website problem, or a change in the portal. Take note of the error message and contact official support if it persists. Never download an unknown app or install remote-access software just because someone claims it is required to check your result.

How to Find the Electricity Bill Reference Number

The reference number is normally printed on the electricity bill near the consumer or billing details. Its position and format can vary between electricity distribution companies, so check the labels on your bill carefully.

Use the reference number connected to the meter and household that applied for the scheme. If you have several bills at the same address, do not guess which one was used. Compare your saved application details or ask official support how to identify the correct connection.

Do not confuse the reference number with the meter serial number, customer ID, account number, or CNIC. If you cannot read the number on a paper bill, obtain a clearer copy through your electricity provider’s official channels.

What If the Electricity Meter Is Not in Your Name?

A meter may be registered under a landlord, family member, or previous resident. The scheme’s eligibility and verification rules determine how such cases are handled. Do not assume that a tenant or household member is automatically eligible simply because they live at the property.

Keep the relevant electricity bill and your own identification available. If a verification team contacts you, ask for official identification and confirm the visit through the appropriate government channel if you are unsure. Do not hand over original documents permanently or share copies with an unverified caller.

What Happens After Selection?

The scheme information describes physical verification by the district administration as part of the process. If your household is selected, follow the instructions provided through verified official channels. Keep your contact information accessible and retain any genuine application or result record.

Before a verification visit, confirm what documents are required through the official programme channel. A genuine process should not require you to pay an unofficial fee to secure a place or guarantee installation. If someone demands money, asks for your banking PIN, or requests an OTP, treat it as a warning sign.

The programme’s installation schedule can change, and older pages may show original target dates. For the latest status of your particular application, rely on current official communication rather than an old news report or social media post.

Common Problems and Practical Solutions

The reference number is rejected

Check every digit and confirm that you are using the correct bill. Avoid adding spaces or punctuation unless the form requests them. If the number is still rejected, contact your electricity provider or scheme support.

The website is not loading

Try again later, refresh the page, or use another browser. Make sure the address is the official one. Do not trust a website simply because it uses the same logo or similar colours.

You cannot find the result section

The portal’s design may have changed. Look for the current result or application section on the official website. If it is not visible, ask the official support channel for the current procedure rather than following an unverified tutorial.

Your saved SMS is missing

If you no longer have the original confirmation message, check any saved application notes or bills. You may still be able to search the result using the details requested on the official portal. Do not pay someone who promises to recover the record through unofficial access.

You suspect a mistake in your details

Contact official support and explain the issue. Keep your bill and identification available, but share them only through a verified channel. Whether a correction is possible depends on the programme’s rules and the stage of processing.

How to Avoid Free Solar Scheme Scams

Government benefit schemes are sometimes used to attract people to fake registration pages or payment requests. Use these precautions:

  1. Use the official domain: Start at cmsolarscheme.punjab.gov.pk.
  2. Do not pay for a result check: Avoid agents who promise selection, approval, or faster installation for money.
  3. Protect your CNIC: Do not post a full CNIC image or number publicly.
  4. Never share OTPs or passwords: A caller claiming to be an official should not need your banking PIN or account password.
  5. Avoid unknown apps: You should not need to install a random APK to check a government result.
  6. Verify messages independently: If a message includes a link, open the official website yourself instead of relying on the message link.
  7. Do not trust guaranteed-selection claims: The original scheme used computerized balloting, and no private agent can legitimately promise to change a result.

If you believe you have been targeted by fraud, preserve the message or transaction evidence and report it through an appropriate official complaint channel.
