Losing 50 to 100 hairs a day is normal. If you are losing clearly more than usual, seeing clumps, bald patches, or a widening part, the cause is usually something specific: stress, illness, nutrition, hormones, medicines, tight hairstyles, scalp problems, or inherited hair loss. Gentle hair care helps, but a lasting increase in hair fall should be checked by a dermatologist.

Source: The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) says people typically shed about 50 to 100 hairs a day. Hard water findings are from published lab studies on hair strength. This article is general information and does not replace medical advice.

Is My Hair Fall Normal or Hair Loss?

Shedding is hair that has finished its growth cycle and falls out. It usually regrows. Hair loss happens when follicles stop producing hair normally, which leads to thinning or a receding hairline.

Signs of normal shedding:

  • Hair in the brush or shower drain, in small amounts
  • No bald patches
  • Hair density looks about the same over months

Signs that need attention:

  • Clumps of hair coming out, or noticeably more than usual
  • A widening part or thinner ponytail
  • Bald patches
  • Itchy, red, painful, or burning scalp

Longer hair makes shedding look worse, and shampooing releases several days of loose hair at once.

What Are the Common Causes of Hair Fall in Pakistan?

Most causes are not specific to Pakistan. Dermatology sources list these as common:

  1. Stress and illness. A major stress, fever, or surgery can trigger shedding a few months later. It usually settles once the trigger resolves.
  2. Nutritional gaps. Low iron, protein, zinc, or biotin can cause noticeable hair loss, and hair can regrow once the problem is treated.
  3. Hormonal changes. Includes thyroid problems, PCOS, and the months after childbirth.
  4. Inherited pattern hair loss. The most common cause worldwide in both men and women.
  5. Medicines. Some drugs can increase shedding.
  6. Tight hairstyles. Tight ponytails, braids, and extensions can pull hair out.
  7. Heat and chemical styling. Frequent straightening, bleaching, and colouring cause breakage.
  8. Scalp conditions. Dandruff and other scalp diseases can affect hair.
  9. Crash diets. Sudden weight loss or very low-calorie diets can trigger shedding.

We could not find a verified Pakistan-specific statistic, so this guide does not quote one.

Does Hard Water Cause Hair Fall in Pakistan?

Probably not at the root, but it can cause breakage. No study has shown that hard water makes hair fall out from the follicle. Lab studies are mixed: one 2013 study found no difference in hair strength between hard and distilled water, while a later study found hair treated with hard water had lower tensile strength.

What hard water may do is leave mineral buildup, making hair dry, tangled, and easier to snap. That can look like hair fall in your brush.

What to do:

  • Rinse hair well so shampoo does not stay on the scalp.
  • Use a gentle clarifying shampoo occasionally, not daily.
  • Try a final rinse with filtered or bottled water if you can.
  • Do not blame water if you see clumps, patches, or scalp symptoms. Get checked.

Does Weather Change Cause Hair Fall?

Seasonal changes can coincide with more shedding, but do not assume the season is the cause. Dermatologists note that persistent or noticeable thinning may signal another condition, so if shedding lasts more than a few weeks, see a doctor.

Can Stress and Anxiety Cause Hair Fall?

Yes. Physical or emotional stress can push many hairs into the shedding phase at once. This is usually temporary, and hair typically returns to its normal growth pattern after the trigger passes. Sleep, regular meals, movement, and support for anxiety all help.

Can Vitamin or Iron Deficiency Cause Hair Fall?

Yes, deficiencies in nutrients such as iron, protein, zinc, or biotin can cause hair loss. But do not self-prescribe high-dose supplements. More of a vitamin or mineral is not necessarily better for hair, and a doctor should confirm what you actually lack first.

What Is a Good Daily Hair Care Routine to Reduce Hair Fall?

  1. Wash 2 to 3 times a week, or as needed. Oily scalps may need more. Use a gentle shampoo.
  2. Condition the lengths, not the scalp.
  3. Detangle gently. Start at the ends, with a wide-tooth comb on damp hair.
  4. Avoid tight hairstyles. Keep ponytails loose and change the parting.
  5. Limit heat and chemicals. Use lower heat and heat protectant.
  6. Use lukewarm water, not very hot.
  7. Sleep on a smooth pillowcase and avoid rollers to bed.

Do Hair Oils and Homemade Masks Stop Hair Fall?

Evidence is limited. Oils such as coconut or mustard oil are common in Pakistani households and can reduce dryness and breakage, but we found no strong evidence that they stop hair fall from the root. Homemade masks (yogurt, egg, aloe) may leave hair feeling softer, not regrow hair.

If you try them:

  • Patch-test on your inner arm first.
  • Do not leave oil on the scalp for days, since buildup can worsen dandruff.
  • Stop if you get itching, redness, or a rash.

Product choice (shampoo or oil) matters less than gentle handling and treating the underlying cause.

Which Foods Help Prevent Hair Fall?

No food reverses hair loss on its own, but a balanced diet supports normal growth. Make sure you get enough:

  • Protein: eggs, dal, chickpeas, fish, chicken, yogurt
  • Iron: lentils, spinach, red meat, with a vitamin C source
  • Zinc: nuts, seeds, whole grains
  • Overall calories: crash diets can trigger shedding

What Mistakes Make Hair Fall Worse?

  1. Brushing wet hair roughly
  2. Tight hairstyles every day
  3. Daily high-heat styling
  4. Frequent bleaching or colouring
  5. Skipping meals or crash dieting
  6. Taking supplements without testing
  7. Switching shampoos every week
  8. Ignoring scalp itching or flaking

How Is Postpartum Hair Fall Handled?

Many women notice more shedding a few months after pregnancy. This is common and usually temporary, since hair returns to its normal cycle. Eat well, be gentle with your hair, and see a doctor if it is severe or lasts beyond several months.

When Should You See a Doctor for Hair Fall?

See a doctor or dermatologist if you notice:

  • Sudden or heavy shedding that continues
  • Clumps of hair or bald patches
  • A widening part or visible thinning
  • Red, itchy, painful, or burning scalp
  • Hair fall with fatigue, weight change, irregular periods, or other symptoms
  • Hair fall that began after a new medicine

A dermatologist may ask about stress, illness, weight changes, pregnancy, and medicines. They may do a pull test and recommend blood tests if needed.

Which Doctor Should You Consult for Hair Fall?

  • Dermatologist (skin and hair specialist): the best first stop
  • Endocrinologist or gynecologist: if a hormonal cause such as thyroid disease or PCOS is suspected
  • Your family doctor: for a general checkup and referral

What Tests May Be Done for Hair Fall?

Your doctor decides, but common checks include a blood count, iron levels, thyroid tests, and sometimes vitamin D or hormone tests, depending on your symptoms. Do not order or interpret tests alone.
